# Bids and tenders

Prompts for winning work and pricing it with fewer surprises.

## Tender clarification log

**Use it for:** turning a first read of tender documents into a clarification log.

~~~
You are an experienced bid manager. Review the tender documents below for [project] and produce a clarification log.

Columns: Ref, Document and section, Issue (ambiguity / conflict / missing information / commercial risk), Question to the client, Impact on price or programme, Priority.

Then list the five clarifications that matter most and why.

Documents: [paste or summarise each document]
~~~

**Check before you use it:**
- Questions do not reveal pricing strategy
- Questions go through the formal tender query route

## Quality submission answer

**Use it for:** a first draft of a quality question answer.

~~~
You are an experienced bid writer for a [contractor / consultancy]. Draft an answer to the tender quality question below.

Question: [paste, including word limit and scoring criteria]
Our relevant experience: [projects, outcomes, people]
Our approach: [key points]
Evidence we can use: [data, case studies, accreditations]

Structure the answer against the scoring criteria. Specific, evidence-led and in the client's language. Stay within [number] words.
~~~

**Check before you use it:**
- Every claim is true and can be evidenced
- Project and client references are approved for use

## Tender return review

**Use it for:** reviewing a subcontract tender return for risk before adjudication.

~~~
You are an experienced UK quantity surveyor. Review this subcontract tender return for [package] against the enquiry.

Report:
1. Departures from the enquiry documents
2. Exclusions and qualifications
3. Programme assumptions
4. Commercial risks (payment terms, retention, liability caps, provisional sums)
5. A short recommendation on whether it is comparable with other returns

Enquiry summary: [paste]
Tender return: [paste]
~~~

**Check before you use it:**
- Every exclusion in the return appears in the output
