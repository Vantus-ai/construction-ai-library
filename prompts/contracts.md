# Contracts

Prompts for understanding and administering construction contracts. These produce a working understanding and draft notices, not legal advice. Always check the actual contract and any amendments.

## Clause explainer

**Use it for:** understanding a clause quickly and what it means for you.

~~~
You are an experienced UK construction contract administrator. Explain the clause below in plain English. I am [the contractor / the employer / a subcontractor / the consultant].

Contract: [NEC4 ECC / JCT D&B 2016 / FIDIC Red Book 2017 / other]

Tell me:
1. What it means
2. When it applies
3. My obligations under it
4. Any notice periods or time bars
5. The risks if I get it wrong
6. Anything I should check in the contract data or amendments

Clause: [paste the full clause text]
~~~

**Check before you use it:**
- You pasted the actual clause text, not just the number
- Amendments and contract data that change the clause were included

## NEC4 early warning notice

**Use it for:** notifying a matter that could increase cost, delay completion or impair performance.

~~~
You are an experienced NEC4 project manager. Draft an early warning notice under NEC4 ECC.

From: [Contractor / Project Manager]
Matter: [what has happened or could happen]
Possible effects: [on cost, completion, key dates and performance]
Proposed actions to avoid or reduce the effect: [suggestions]
Request: an early warning meeting, if appropriate

Then draft the matching early warning register entry with columns: Ref, Description, Possible effect, Actions, Owner, Date for action.

Factual and neutral. No admissions of fault.
~~~

**Check before you use it:**
- The notice goes through the communication method set out in the contract
- The register entry matches the notice

## NEC4 compensation event notification

**Use it for:** notifying an event you believe is a compensation event.

~~~
You are an experienced NEC4 commercial manager acting for the Contractor. Draft a notification of a compensation event under NEC4 ECC.

Event: [what happened, when, and who instructed it if relevant]
Date the Contractor became aware: [date]
Reason it is a compensation event: [the event type if known, otherwise identify the most likely and flag for checking]
Early warning given: [yes, with reference / no]

State the facts only. Do not include a quotation. Note that a quotation will follow when instructed. Under 250 words.
~~~

**Check before you use it:**
- The notification is within the time bar in the contract (check the date of awareness carefully)
- The compensation event type is correct for your contract and Z clauses

## JCT payment and pay less notice check

**Use it for:** checking a payment notice or pay less notice for basic validity issues before you rely on it or respond.

~~~
You are an experienced UK quantity surveyor familiar with JCT contracts and the Housing Grants, Construction and Regeneration Act 1996 as amended. Review the notice below.

Contract: [JCT D&B 2016 / JCT SBC 2016 / other]
Due date: [date]
Final date for payment: [date]
Notice type: [payment notice / pay less notice]
Notice received or issued on: [date]

Check and report on:
1. Whether it appears to be issued within the required timescale
2. Whether it states the sum considered due and the basis of calculation
3. Any missing information
4. Questions to raise with the other party or with legal advisers

Notice: [paste]
~~~

**Check before you use it:**
- Dates come from the contract and payment schedule, not memory
- Anything with a dispute in prospect goes to a qualified adviser

## FIDIC claim notice

**Use it for:** a first notice of claim under a FIDIC 2017 contract.

~~~
You are an experienced contract administrator working on a FIDIC [Red / Yellow / Silver] Book 2017 contract in [country]. Draft a notice of claim from [the Contractor / the Employer] to the Engineer.

Event or circumstance: [description]
Date first aware, or should have been aware: [date]
Entitlement sought: [extension of time / additional payment / both]
Supporting records being kept: [list]

Describe the event and state that a fully detailed claim will follow. Do not quantify unless asked. Under 250 words.
~~~

**Check before you use it:**
- The notice period in the contract and particular conditions is met
- Local law and any particular conditions that change claim procedure have been checked
