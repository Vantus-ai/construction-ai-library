# Project delivery

Prompts for the documents that keep a project moving.

## Request for information (RFI)

**Use it for:** turning a site issue or design gap into a clear, answerable question.

~~~
You are an experienced UK project manager. Draft an RFI to [the architect / the structural engineer / the M&E consultant / the client].

Project: [name or reference]
Location: [level, gridline, room or zone]
Drawings and documents referenced: [drawing numbers, revisions, specification clauses]
Issue: [what is unclear, conflicting or missing]
Impact if not resolved: [on programme, cost, safety or quality]
Response needed by: [date and why]

Write: a short subject line, a one-paragraph description, numbered questions that can each be answered clearly, and a proposed solution if we have one. Under 250 words.
~~~

**Check before you use it:**
- Every drawing reference and revision is correct
- Each question can be answered with a decision, not a discussion
- The response date reflects the real programme need

## Meeting minutes and action log

**Use it for:** turning notes or a transcript into minutes people will read.

~~~
You are an experienced project coordinator. Turn the notes below into formal minutes for [meeting type, eg progress meeting 7].

Attendees: [names and organisations]
Date: [date]

Produce:
1. A summary of decisions made
2. Minutes by agenda item, short and factual
3. An action log table: Ref, Action, Owner, Due date, Status
4. Items carried forward to the next meeting

Do not invent decisions, owners or dates. If an action has no owner or date, mark it "TBC".

Notes: [paste notes or transcript]
~~~

**Check before you use it:**
- Every action has the right owner
- Nothing contentious is recorded more firmly than it was agreed

## Programme narrative

**Use it for:** the monthly programme commentary for a client or board.

~~~
You are an experienced UK planner. Write the programme narrative for [project] for the period ending [date].

Planned completion: [date]
Forecast completion: [date]
Critical path: [description]
Activities completed this period: [list]
Activities behind programme and why: [list]
Mitigation in place: [list]
Key risks to the programme: [list]

Structure: headline position, progress this period, critical path and float, delays and mitigation, risks, look-ahead to next period. Maximum 350 words.
~~~

**Check before you use it:**
- Dates match the current programme revision
- Delay causes are described factually, without accepting liability

## Site diary summary

**Use it for:** summarising a week of site diaries into a progress and issues report.

~~~
You are a site manager reporting to the project director. Summarise the site diary entries below for the week ending [date].

Produce:
1. Progress by area or trade
2. Labour and plant levels, with any shortfalls
3. Weather or access issues and their effect
4. Instructions received and from whom
5. Health and safety observations
6. Issues that may lead to delay or cost, with dates

Keep the facts, dates and names exactly as recorded. Under 400 words.

Diary entries: [paste]
~~~

**Check before you use it:**
- Nothing that may support a later claim has been summarised away
- Dates and instruction sources are exactly as recorded

## Lessons learned

**Use it for:** capturing lessons at stage completion or project close.

~~~
You are a project director running a lessons learned review. From the notes below, produce a lessons learned register.

Columns: Ref, Stage, What happened, Impact, Root cause, What we would do differently, Owner for future projects.

Then summarise the three lessons with the biggest impact and one change to our standard process for each.

Notes: [paste]
~~~

**Check before you use it:**
- Root causes describe processes, not individuals
- Each lesson has a practical change attached
