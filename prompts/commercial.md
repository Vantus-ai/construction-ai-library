# Commercial

Prompts for the commercial documents that take time every month.

## Variation notice

**Use it for:** formally notifying a change to the scope of works.

~~~
You are an experienced UK quantity surveyor. Draft a formal variation notice to [the employer / the main contractor] notifying a change to the scope of works.

Change: [what changed, who instructed it and when]
Estimated value: [amount, or "to be confirmed pending quotation"]
Programme impact: [known impact, or "under assessment"]
Contract: [JCT D&B 2016 / NEC4 ECC / FIDIC Red Book 2017 / other]
Relevant clause: [clause if known, otherwise identify the most likely clause and flag it for checking]

Tone: formal and factual. Do not commit to a value or programme impact that has not been agreed. Under 250 words.
~~~

**Check before you use it:**
- The clause is right for your contract and any amendments
- Nothing commits you commercially beyond what you would put in writing
- Any notice period or time bar is identified and met

## Cost report narrative

**Use it for:** the monthly narrative that sits on top of the cost report.

~~~
You are a UK cost manager writing for a client board. Write the monthly cost report narrative.

Project: [type and scale, eg 120-unit residential new build]
Contract sum: [amount]
Forecast final account: [amount]
Movement since last report: [amount up or down]
Main movements and reasons: [list each one]
Risks and opportunities not yet in the forecast: [list]

Structure: headline position, key movements, risks and opportunities, next steps. Plain English a client board will understand. Maximum 300 words.
~~~

Follow up with: "Now give me a three-line version for the cover email."

**Check before you use it:**
- Every figure matches the report itself
- Movements add up to the total movement
- Nothing is described as agreed that is still under negotiation

## Subcontractor quote comparison

**Use it for:** comparing two or more quotes for the same package before you compare on price.

~~~
You are an experienced UK quantity surveyor. Compare these subcontractor quotes for [package, eg drylining and ceilings].

Produce a table with columns: Item, Subcontractor A, Subcontractor B, Comment.
Then list separately:
1. Exclusions in each quote
2. Qualifications and assumptions
3. Items in one quote but missing from the other
4. Questions to ask each subcontractor before the quotes can be compared on price

Quote A: [paste]
Quote B: [paste]
~~~

**Check before you use it:**
- Company names are replaced with Subcontractor A and B
- Every exclusion in the source documents appears in the output
- The clarification questions cover programme, attendance and preliminaries

## Payment application cover letter

**Use it for:** the letter that accompanies an interim payment application.

~~~
You are a UK quantity surveyor. Draft a cover letter to accompany interim payment application [number] dated [date].

Gross valuation: [amount]
Retention: [percentage and amount]
Previously certified: [amount]
Amount due this period: [amount]
Works in the period: [brief description]
Contract and clause: [eg JCT D&B 2016, clause 4.7]

Formal tone. Request assessment within the contractual timeframe. Maximum 200 words.
~~~

**Check before you use it:**
- The arithmetic is correct
- Dates align with the contract payment timetable

## Final account summary

**Use it for:** summarising the final account position for a client or internal review.

~~~
You are a senior UK quantity surveyor. Summarise the final account position for [project].

Original contract sum: [amount]
Agreed variations: [total and count]
Variations under negotiation: [total and count]
Claims or disputes: [summary]
Proposed final account: [amount]

Produce: a one-paragraph summary, a table of agreed and outstanding items by category, the key risks to closing the account and a recommended route to agreement. Under 400 words.
~~~

**Check before you use it:**
- Agreed and outstanding items reconcile to the proposed final account
- The recommended route reflects the actual commercial relationship
